## Problem

AI Agents exhibited **"Phantom Reconstruction"**—autonomously generating misformatted governance reports in the **Ephemeral Scratchpad** without current context. This violated write restrictions and revealed an underlying cognitive bias.

## Investigation

1. **Git Status Audit**: Found that deleted files remained in an "Untracked" or "Staged" state in the version control system.
2. **History Analysis**: Identified **Polluted History Snapshots** containing problematic content. A **Global Reset Operation** left semantic debris in subsequent fragmented commits.

### Core Discovery: Non-Intuitive Pollution Source

Pollution stems from **Version Control History (Git Log)**. AI Agents scan history for context; orphaned paths after a reset were misinterpreted as "lost development intent," triggering an "auto-repair" instinct.

## Finding

### 1. Instruction Hallucination Mechanism
AI Agents treat history commits as valid context to establish consistency. Polluted fragments induce "Reconstruction" even after file deletion. This is a **"Path-Dependent Cognitive Bias."**

### 2. Solution: Physical Enforcement
Simple deletion is insufficient. Effective solutions require:
- **Logical Reset**: Eliminate fragmented records.
- **Physical Purge**: Force-sync to remove polluted records from both cloud and local history.

## Key Lessons

- **History as Instruction**: To AI, version control history is a powerful instruction source.
- **Reset is Not Disappearance**: Fragmented history is a breeding ground for cognitive pollution.
- **Physical Constraints over Attention**: Physically removing polluted material is the only radical cure.
