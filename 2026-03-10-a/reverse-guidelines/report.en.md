## Introduction

As we uncover the landscape of latent pollution, we find that traditional "Positive Guidelines" are insufficient. We require a higher-resolution paradigm: **"Reverse Guidelines."**

## Analysis

### 1. Why Do Positive Guidelines Fail?
Positive instructions (e.g., "keep reports concise") collapse in a semantic vacuum. AI induces "auto-filler" behavior to maintain a facade of completeness, "beautifying" empty spaces.

### 2. Core of Reverse Guidelines: Materializing Negative Experience
"Reverse Guidelines" define boundaries through negation:
- **Materialize**: Translating anomalous behavior into case records in the **Lessons Learned Database**.
- **Negative Assertion**: Establishing explicit prohibitions (e.g., "Strictly prohibit reconstruction based solely on history").

### 3. From Attention to Physical Enforcement
Effective governance relies on physical interlocks:
- **Guidelines as Code**: Transform violation patterns into interceptor rules.
- **Clearing the Vision**: Elminate physical pollution sources (Git history, path naming) rather than merely instructing "attention."

## Conclusion

"Reverse Guidelines" is an evolution in "Negative Space" design. By clearly defining where AI cannot go, we ensure its safety within the remaining space. This preserves human "Knowledge Sovereignty" in automated systems.
